01 / THE CALCULATION

How the estimates are calculated.

Each prompt has an expected output length. We approximate one token as 0.75 words and apply Epoch AI’s 2025 estimate for GPT-4o: 0.3 Wh per 500 output tokens. The figure is an analytical estimate based on hardware and workload assumptions. [1]

Water and carbon use fixed ratios derived from Google’s rounded Gemini Apps reference: 0.26 ÷ 0.24 mL/Wh and 0.03 ÷ 0.24 g CO₂e/Wh. The calculator combines these ratios with the GPT-4o energy estimate as a hypothetical scenario. No chatbot is queried or measured by this site. The carbon ratio uses market-based accounting, not a local grid emission factor. [2]

CALCULATION
  1. TokensExpected words ÷ 0.75
  2. ElectricityTokens × 0.3 ÷ 500 Wh
  3. Cooling waterElectricity × (0.26 ÷ 0.24) mL
  4. EmissionsElectricity × (0.03 ÷ 0.24) g CO₂e

Each result is multiplied by responses per person and group size: 1, 30, 200 or 10,000 people.

Actual use depends on the model, language, hardware and workload; energy per token can also change with output length. Long documents are linear extrapolations and may require several model calls. Training, user devices, networks, PDF processing, large input contexts, hidden reasoning and retries are not separately modelled. [7]

03 / EVERYDAY EQUIVALENTS

Everyday comparisons.

Water

A glass represents a chosen 250 mL. Below 7.6 L, the calculator shows glasses; from 7.6 L upwards, it shows shower time at an assumed 7.6 L/min, the approximate WaterSense maximum. [6]

Electricity

TV time uses a 100 W television, the comparison in Google’s study. Hours of viewing = Wh ÷ 100. [2]

Carbon

Car distance uses 142 g CO₂e/km for Impact CO₂’s average diesel-car reference, including manufacture and use. [5]

These compare quantities, not identical environmental effects. Cooling-water consumption and household water use differ; scarcity depends on location. Shower heating is excluded. [3]

04 / THE ETHICAL QUESTION

Environmental costs and business responsibility.

01

Local water use.

AI’s users and the communities supplying water and electricity may be far apart. Businesses should consider local scarcity and community needs alongside the value created by their services. [3]

02

Supplier transparency.

Supplier figures can cover different activities. Procurement decisions need clear boundaries and assumptions. Reducing cooling water can also increase electricity demand, so one “green” score can hide trade-offs. [2] [3]

03

Total demand.

Efficiency gains do not guarantee lower total demand if use grows faster. Businesses should track total electricity and cooling-water use alongside consumption per response, and avoid generating material they will not use. [4]
